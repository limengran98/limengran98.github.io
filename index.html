My research spans AI for Science, heterogeneous graph learning, hypergraphs, temporal modeling, and bioinformatics. My recent work studies measurement sufficiency, model revision through execution feedback, and input-use auditing in cellular-response models.

I am advised by Prof. Ronghui Zhang and Prof. Yong Zhang. Previously, I interned at Baidu, SIAT, Westlake University, and CAIR.

CellScientistPreprint · 2026

CellScientist: From Execution Feedback to Auditable Model-Revision Trajectories for Cellular Perturbation Prediction

Mengran Li, Bo Li, Jiaying Wang, Wenbin Xing, Chengyang Zhang, Jinlin Wu, Zhen Lei, Jiebo Luo, Stan Z. Li, Zelin Zang

CellScientist converts execution and validation feedback into local model revisions under a fixed task contract, preserving successful updates, regressions, rejected candidates, and failures as auditable trajectories for cellular perturbation prediction across morphological, transcriptomic, and single-cell response spaces.

CellAuditPreprint · 2026

Discover, Falsify, Revise: Auditing Input-Use Claims from Source Code to Predictive Contribution in Agent-Discovered Cell Models

Mengran Li, Bo Li, Chengyang Zhang, Yang Yan, Jinfeng Xu, Zhenchao Tang

CellAudit traces input-use claims from source code to fitted-model dependence and target-relevant predictive contribution, revealing when strong cellular-response prediction masks unused perturbation inputs and using falsification evidence to guide model revision and test claim generalization across independently acquired cohorts.

APPIEEE TNNLS 2026

Adaptive Prototype-Guided Personalized Propagation for Heterophilic Graphs With Missing Data

Mengran Li, Wenbin Xing, Zelin Zang, Bo Li, Chengyang Zhang, Yong Zhang, Junzhou Chen, Ronghui Zhang, Yongfu Li, Chuan Hu, Xiaolei Ma, Zibin Zheng

We propose APP, a unified framework for learning on heterophilic graphs with missing features. By introducing global semantic prototypes, virtual edge propagation, and adaptive fusion, APP jointly addresses propagation distortion and feature imputation, achieving robust performance under the challenging heterophily-missing coupling (HMC) setting.

Mengran Li, Zelin Zang, Wenbin Xing, Junzhou Chen, Ronghui Zhang, Jiebo Luo, Stan Z. Li

We propose CHMR, a framework that jointly models local-global dependencies between molecules and cellular responses. By using a tree-structured vector quantization, we capture latent biological hierarchies, significantly improving molecular property prediction.
